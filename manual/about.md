## About the HORA Abeshu Research Group

The HORA Abeshu Research Group studies hydrologic systems, water infrastructure, and coupled human-water processes across watershed, regional, and Earth-system scales.

We develop data-driven, physics-informed, and AI-enabled methods for resilient, equitable, and operationally intelligent water systems. Our work connects hydroclimate extremes, infrastructure operations, Earth observation, lake and reservoir dynamics, water equity, and Earth-system modeling.

The group is based at New Mexico State University and works at the boundary between hydrology, civil and environmental engineering, artificial intelligence, infrastructure systems, and decision science.

## Our Values

There are a few values that should shape how we work:

- A respectful, constructive, and welcoming research environment where people can ask questions early.
- High standards and kindness at the same time. Students should feel comfortable asking basic questions, challenging assumptions, and admitting uncertainty.
- Research that improves understanding and supports decisions for water systems, communities, and the environment.
- Rigorous science that can be inspected, reproduced, challenged, and improved.
- Slow, careful thinking when the problem requires it, especially when claims affect communities, infrastructure, or policy.
- Open-source software and open research practices where possible.
- Clear documentation, because undocumented knowledge does not transfer well.
- Healthy academic work habits. Urgency happens, but constant urgency is a planning failure.

## Your Digital Identity as a Scientist

A modern research profile is partly digital. New members should create or update:

- ORCID profile
- GitHub account
- Google Scholar profile when publications are available
- Professional homepage or institutional profile when appropriate
- LinkedIn profile if it helps with internships, collaborations, or job searches
- A consistent name format for publications and repositories

ORCID is especially important because journals, professional societies, funding agencies, and universities use it to connect researchers with their work.

## Our Community

HORA members may engage with different scientific communities depending on their projects. Common communities include:

- American Geophysical Union for hydrology, climate, Earth observation, and water resources.
- American Society of Civil Engineers and EWRI for civil infrastructure, hydrology, hydraulics, and water management.
- Consortium of Universities for the Advancement of Hydrologic Science for hydrologic data, modeling, and community cyberinfrastructure.
- International Association of Hydrological Sciences for hydrology and global water science.
- American Meteorological Society for hydroclimate, weather, and climate-risk topics.
- INFORMS for optimization, operations research, and decision analytics.
- AI-for-science and machine-learning communities for physics-informed learning, uncertainty, and model diagnostics.
- Local and regional water management communities connected to New Mexico, the Southwest, and partner study regions.

## To Do List for New Members

- Read the public HORA website and this manual.
- Create or update your ORCID profile.
- Set up a GitHub account and share your username with the group.
- Identify one or two professional societies most relevant to your research.
- Read recent HORA publications or project notes related to your topic.
- Start a project README early, even if the project is still forming.
- Schedule a short conversation about your research goals, expected outputs, and support needs.
