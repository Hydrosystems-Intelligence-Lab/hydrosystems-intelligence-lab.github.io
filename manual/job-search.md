## Start Early

Job and fellowship applications take longer than expected. Build materials before a deadline is active:

- CV
- Short research statement
- Teaching or mentoring statement when relevant
- Diversity, equity, inclusion, and belonging statement when requested
- One-page project summary
- Website or portfolio
- Reference list

## Academic Applications

Academic applications often require a coherent research vision. A strong research statement should explain:

- What problem area you own
- Why it matters now
- What methods make your work distinctive
- What you will do in the next three to five years
- Why the target department or program fits

## Nonacademic Applications

For agency, utility, nonprofit, national lab, and industry roles, translate research into capabilities:

- Data analysis
- Modeling
- Forecasting
- Geospatial workflows
- Scientific programming
- Uncertainty analysis
- Stakeholder communication
- Decision support

## Fellowships

Keep a running list of fellowship deadlines. Start drafts early enough to get feedback on framing, not just proofreading.

## References

Ask for letters early. Provide the recommender with the call, deadline, CV, draft materials, and a short note explaining what you hope the letter emphasizes.
