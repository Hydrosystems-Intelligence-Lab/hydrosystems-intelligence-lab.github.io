## Overview

The advisor is an academic and professional mentor, not a boss or supervisor. Graduate students do not work for the advisor; they work with the advisor on shared scientific goals.

The advisor is committed to:

- providing guidance on research, coursework, and professional development;
- supporting funding pathways through fellowships, research assistantships, and teaching assistantships; and
- helping students prepare for and pursue post-PhD opportunities.

This document outlines how the HORA Abeshu Research Group operates. It is not exhaustive and will evolve as the group grows. Students are encouraged to ask questions about anything that is unclear.

## Meetings

### Frequency

All students should expect a weekly individual meeting at a regularly scheduled time. As students progress, the cadence may shift to a more flexible schedule by mutual agreement.

### Format

Meetings are typically in person when both parties are on campus, and by video call otherwise. Students should come with an agenda, an update on progress since the last meeting, and questions or decisions they need input on.

### Group meetings

All group members are expected to attend the weekly HORA Abeshu Research Group meeting, where members present research progress, discuss new papers, and prepare for upcoming conferences and proposals.

## Degree Progress

Progress is monitored through:

- ongoing advisor-student discussions;
- the annual review required by the Department of Civil and Environmental Engineering;
- annual meetings with the dissertation committee (after committee formation);
- the Qualifying Exam, the Dissertation Proposal Defense, and the Final Defense.

Students are encouraged to refer to the HORA Abeshu PhD Progress Rubric (separate document) for stage-by-stage expectations across research, teaching, coursework, and professional development.

## Dissertation Committee

The dissertation committee should be formed by the end of the first year. Students are expected to consult closely with the advisor on committee composition. The advisor has institutional context about individual faculty that is valuable for this decision.

The committee meets at minimum annually to discuss research progress, plans, coursework, and professional goals. The committee also conducts the Qualifying Exam, the Proposal Defense, and the Final Defense.

## Time Off, Vacation, and Work Hours

Graduate students are independent adults responsible for managing their own time. Students are expected to commit the time and effort needed to make research progress, including any progress required by their funding source. Within that, students should find the schedule and routine that works best for them.

Vacation, holidays, and personal time are encouraged. It is courteous to inform the advisor of planned absences. Coordination becomes important when manuscripts are under review at journals, since journal deadlines can appear unexpectedly.

## Coursework

Students are expected to consult with the advisor about course selection. Backgrounds and career goals vary, so the right courses differ for each student. The dissertation committee may also recommend or require specific courses, and the advisor is responsible for ensuring those recommendations are met. The final choice of courses rests with the student, but the conversation matters.

## Writing and Feedback

Students are encouraged to share drafts with the advisor early and often, beginning at very early stages and continuing through journal submission.

- Early drafts will receive structural feedback (sections, paragraphs, framing).
- Once structure is solid, drafts will receive detailed line-level feedback.
- Detailed feedback is time-intensive (about 15 minutes per page; about 8 hours for a full manuscript). Students should refine their prose with each iteration.
- Students should not send drafts to co-authors until there is mutual agreement with the advisor on the version to circulate.

## Letters of Recommendation

For the first letter at a given career stage (graduate fellowship, postdoc, faculty position), please give at least four weeks notice when possible. If something arises on a shorter timeline, ask anyway; flexibility is possible.

For subsequent letters at the same career stage, one week notice is sufficient (more is appreciated).

When requesting a letter, provide:

- the position or fellowship description;
- submission deadline and method;
- your most recent CV;
- a short note on what you would like emphasized.

## Funding and Fellowships

The advisor is responsible for ensuring the department's commitment to PhD funding is met (typically through a combination of fellowships, research assistantships, and teaching assistantships). Because grants are usually shorter than the PhD timeline, the exact mix of funding sources will evolve through your time in the program. Expect to be involved in this process through fellowship applications and grant preparation.

Students are expected to apply for relevant external fellowships (for example: NSF GRFP for eligible students, NASA FINESST, DOE Computational Science Graduate Fellowship, and Ford Foundation Fellowship). Beyond financial support, the application process itself is professionally valuable.

## Conference Travel and Additional Funds

Students should expect funding for at least one conference per year, with additional support possible depending on circumstances and grant constraints. Priority for additional travel typically goes to senior students preparing for the post-PhD job search. Students should always apply for available travel awards (AGU, CUAHSI, and university-level).

## Presentations

Presentation is a core part of scientific work and professional development. Students should expect to present in:

- HORA Abeshu Research Group meetings;
- NMSU CEE seminars and the IAAM seminar series;
- annual NMSU Graduate Research Symposium;
- AGU Fall Meeting and other field-specific conferences (CUAHSI Biennial, AMS, NeurIPS climate workshops, and related venues).

## Publications and Authorship

Students are expected to submit dissertation chapters for publication in peer-reviewed journals as each chapter matures. Dissertations in the HORA Abeshu Research Group will typically consist of an overarching introduction and conclusion, plus three to four chapters of original research, each at some point in the publication process.

The default expectation is that the advisor is a co-author on dissertation chapters, acting as a highly engaged collaborator. Other co-authors will be added per standard authorship practices in hydrology, Earth system science, and machine learning.

Discuss authorship early, ideally before analysis begins, and certainly before writing begins. Authorship discussions are ongoing, not one-time. When in doubt, raise it.

## Ready to Graduate

The PhD signifies the capability to independently initiate, conduct, document, and defend original scholarship. Graduation is determined primarily by research maturity, along with preparation for professional goals. Funding constraints and post-PhD opportunities also factor into the conversation.

In the HORA Abeshu Research Group, the typical expectation at defense is that the student has at least one chapter published or in press, at least one additional chapter submitted, and the remaining chapters in advanced form.

The final decision on graduation timing rests with the dissertation committee.

## Other People You Should Consult

It is critical to have multiple mentors and intellectual influences. Beyond the advisor, students are encouraged to build mentoring relationships with:

- members of the dissertation committee;
- other CEE and IAAM faculty;
- faculty at other NMSU colleges (Engineering, Arts and Sciences, ACES);
- external collaborators at PNNL, UH, and partner institutions;
- postdocs and senior students within the group;
- members of the broader scientific community (AGU, CUAHSI, Earth Science Women's Network, BlackInGeo, Latinx in STEM, and related communities).

Institutional resources include the CEE Director of Graduate Studies, the CEE Department Head, the NMSU Graduate School, NMSU Counseling and Psychological Services, and the NMSU Ombudsperson.

## Topics Beyond Research

Students are welcome to discuss any topic they find appropriate: teaching opportunities, career planning, work-life balance, personal challenges, immigration and visa issues, family considerations, and anything else relevant to their well-being and growth.

## Respectful Community

All members of the HORA Abeshu Research Group are expected to adhere to NMSU standards of conduct and to treat advisors, peers, collaborators, and community partners with respect. The group has zero tolerance for harassment, discrimination, or behavior that undermines the dignity of any community member.
