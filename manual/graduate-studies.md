## Ownership of Progress

Graduate students are expected to take active ownership of their degree progress. This includes understanding program requirements, tracking deadlines, preparing for meetings, and communicating early when something is unclear or blocked.

## Advising Meetings

Use regular meetings to make decisions, not just report activity. A useful update includes:

- What changed since the last meeting.
- What result, figure, dataset, or idea needs discussion.
- What decision is needed.
- What will happen before the next meeting.

## Milestones

Track the requirements for your specific degree program. Milestones are typical planning markers, not guaranteed timelines. They should be adjusted based on official program requirements, project development, coursework, funding context, and student goals. Common milestones include:

- Coursework plan
- Committee formation
- Qualifying or comprehensive exam
- Proposal or prospectus
- Annual progress review
- Manuscript submissions
- Thesis or dissertation defense

## Reading

Build the habit of reading every week. Keep a living literature map with:

- Core papers
- Methods papers
- Data sources
- Open questions
- Papers you disagree with and why

## Professional Development

Graduate study should build both scientific depth and professional range. Consider opportunities in:

- Fellowships
- Conference presentations
- Teaching and mentoring
- Open-source software
- Data publication
- Agency or utility collaboration
- Internships
- Interdisciplinary working groups

## Communication

Communicate early about blockers, uncertainty, authorship questions, travel plans, deadline conflicts, and changes in scope. Students are not expected to solve every problem alone, but they are expected to describe what they have tried, where they are stuck, and what kind of help would be useful.
